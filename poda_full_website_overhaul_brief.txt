PODA CAPITAL — FULL WEBSITE OVERHAUL BRIEF FOR CLAUDE CODE

You are reconfiguring the entire existing Poda Capital website. Treat this as a full strategic and visual overhaul—not a light reskin.

First inspect the complete repository, identify the current framework, routes, components, styling system, content/data structure, assets, and any working functionality. Preserve useful infrastructure and adapt the implementation to the current stack. Do not replace the framework, introduce unnecessary dependencies, or destroy working integrations unless the current implementation makes the new experience impossible.

Before editing:

1. Audit the repository and summarize the existing architecture.
2. Identify which components and functionality can be preserved.
3. Create a short implementation plan.
4. Then execute the complete redesign.
5. After implementation, run the project, resolve errors, and verify every route at desktop and mobile sizes.

Do not stop after the audit or provide mock code only. Make the changes directly in the repository.

# 1. THE NEW PODA

Poda is an independent, selective fashion retailer built around culturally informed product selections.

Core positioning:

“Poda identifies what is moving, explains why it matters, and builds a store around its strongest expression.”

Category:

SELECTIVE RETAIL / MARKET INTELLIGENCE
NEW YORK + WASHINGTON, DC

Poda is not:

- A general fashion marketplace
- A SaaS closet-management platform
- A user-uploaded closet app
- A mass consignment platform
- A personal portfolio tracker
- A conventional fashion blog
- A store with hundreds of disconnected products
- A generic luxury moodboard
- A streetwear resale clone

Remove, archive, or fully de-emphasize the former “Personal Closet OS,” “Closet Capital,” portfolio dashboard, public user profiles, open closet uploads, investment-style closet valuations, and similar concepts. Do not allow remnants of the old product direction to confuse the new site.

Poda’s system is:

- Market Notes are the reasoning.
- The Lookbook is the world.
- The Edit is the store.
- The Sourcing Desk is the conversation.

The larger operating sequence is:

MARKET NOTE → THESIS → VISUAL STUDY → THE EDIT → DROP → SOURCING DESK → REVIEW

Every section should feel connected to one current thesis. Research should lead to imagery. Imagery should lead to products. Products and unanswered demand should lead to sourcing requests. Customer behavior should help define the next thesis.

# 2. BUSINESS MODEL

Poda begins with narrowly curated, permissioned inventory sourced from:

- Emerging and independent brands
- Selected vintage sellers
- Individual collectors and personal closets
- Occasional Poda-owned inventory later

Initial supply mix:

- Emerging brands: approximately 40–50%
- Vintage sellers: approximately 25–35%
- Individual closets: approximately 15–25%

Initial operating rules:

- Zero owned-inventory exposure wherever possible
- Products remain with the brand, seller, or owner until sold unless otherwise arranged
- Poda controls selection, presentation, pricing recommendations, and markdown timing
- Poda only presents pieces that it has permission to promote or sell
- Initial drops should contain approximately 8–15 products
- Initial target is 10–12 strong products from approximately two brands and two vintage sellers
- Core price range: $175–$600
- Initial concentration: $200–$450
- Avoid filling the store with inexpensive products that create substantial handling work without sufficient contribution
- Each item should generate at least approximately $40 of revenue for Poda
- The assortment should feel scarce and deliberate

The website must support different transaction types without visually fragmenting the store:

1. Poda sale — Product can be purchased directly through Poda.
2. Assisted purchase — Customer submits an inquiry or messages Poda to complete the purchase.
3. Partner purchase — Product links to an approved external retailer or seller.
4. Sourcing request — Product is not currently available, but the customer can ask Poda to locate it or something similar.

Build the product data model so each item can have a transaction type and the correct CTA appears automatically.

Do not build a fake checkout if the current repository has no real commerce infrastructure. If checkout does not exist, create a polished inquiry-led purchase flow that can later be connected to Shopify, Stripe, or another backend.

# 3. BRAND AND VISUAL DIRECTION

The site should feel like a combination of:

- An internal research memo
- A highly selective independent retailer
- A cultural archive
- A controlled editorial publication
- A slightly dated corporate digital system made intentional

The visual identity is “dark corporate,” monochrome, editorial, and email-inspired.

It should feel intelligent, sparse, restrained, and specific. It should not look like a generic Shopify luxury template.

Use:

- Black, near-black, charcoal, bone, off-white, muted grey
- Restrained accent color only when functionally useful
- Thin borders
- Dense but controlled metadata
- Strong grids
- Editorial image crops
- Small uppercase utility labels
- Large, clean thesis statements
- Occasional timestamps, issue numbers, item numbers, statuses, and category labels
- Email, memo, terminal, research-database, or archive references used subtly
- Generous negative space
- A balance between analytical structure and strong fashion imagery

Avoid:

- Gradients
- Glassmorphism
- Rounded cards everywhere
- Excessive pill buttons
- Huge generic luxury serif headlines
- Empty “quiet luxury” clichés
- Neon cyberpunk styling
- Crypto or finance-dashboard aesthetics
- Stock photography
- Generic beige minimalism
- Constant animations
- Anything that resembles a fashion-tech SaaS landing page

Typography should combine a clean grotesk/sans-serif with a mono or technical supporting typeface. Use serif typography only if it adds a meaningful editorial contrast. Use fonts already available in the project where suitable; otherwise use accessible web-safe or open-source choices without adding excessive loading overhead.

Motion should be restrained: fast fades, controlled image reveals, subtle underline or border changes, and minimal hover movement. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
