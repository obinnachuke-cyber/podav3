The business model

One storefront, many owners

Poda publishes selected pieces from independent suppliers: private closets, independent boutiques, vintage resellers and other independent fashion sellers. Poda decides what appears on the storefront.

Each piece stays tied to the supplier that owns it until it sells. When it sells, Poda keeps a commission and the supplier is owed the rest.

Terms can differ by supplier and by piece. The rate that applies is copied onto the order at the moment of purchase, so a later change to terms never rewrites a sale already made.

The operating problem is coordination. Several independent suppliers share one storefront, and Poda has to keep inventory accurate, know which supplier each sale belongs to, and know what it owes each of them.

Product decisions

Decisions Behind the System

Curated supply

Poda controls what appears on the storefront. Suppliers submit, and nothing is public until Poda has accepted a piece and placed it in a release. Accepting a piece does not publish it. The drop time does.

Supplier ownership

Every product carries the supplier that owns it, and the database refuses to change that. A supplier sees only its own pieces and cannot edit its own commission or status. Ownership is what makes attribution reliable at sale time.

Variable commissions

Starting rates differ by type: 25 percent for consignors, 15 percent for brands and boutiques, and 0 for Poda's own pieces. Any supplier can be given its own rate, and one piece can carry an override. Rates are stored in basis points so every calculation stays in whole cents.

Centralized operations

Review, releases, orders, shipping, refunds and payouts sit in one admin area. Each decision is made against the same records, so the payout screen never disagrees with the orders screen.

Limited inventory exposure

Poda does not buy a piece before it sells. Ownership stays with the supplier until a customer pays, so the business starts without a large inventory purchase. The cost is dependence on suppliers, which is why checkout holds a piece for 30 minutes and flags any payment that arrives for a piece already gone.

Editorial distribution

Pieces reach customers through releases. A release pairs its products with a Market Note and Index entries, so the reason a piece is in the drop sits next to the piece. Inventory choices and storytelling are made together.

The next commercial opportunity

Where I'm Taking It Next

Poda began as a curated consumer marketplace. Building its operating model raised a second question: whether the consignment infrastructure underneath is useful to people who run consignment relationships with no software at all.

Many smaller sellers handle other people's inventory through spreadsheets, messages, notes and manual payment tracking. That works for a few consignors. The work grows with every added consignor, piece and sale.

My next step is to explore whether Poda's internal consignment system can serve these operators directly.

Who might use it

  • Independent fashion resellers
  • Personal shoppers
  • Vintage sellers
  • Closet managers
  • Small consignment operators
  • Individuals selling merchandise on behalf of other people
  • Sellers managing inventory from several independent owners

A possible product

A lightweight supplier and consignment management system that lets an independent seller:

  1. Create supplier or consignor profiles
  2. Record inventory ownership
  3. Upload and manage consigned products
  4. Set commission terms by supplier
  5. Track products from listing to sale
  6. Attribute completed sales to the correct owner
  7. Calculate commissions and seller earnings
  8. Keep a record of amounts owed
  9. Track payment and payout status
  10. Generate supplier statements and reconciliation records

The aim is to make consignment administration manageable for independent operators without asking them to adopt a complex retail management platform.

How I plan to validate it

This is an opportunity under exploration, not an established product with proven demand. I have no evidence yet about market size, competing tools or willingness to pay. The plan is to find out in this order:

  1. Keep operating Poda's own consignment marketplace.
  2. Speak with smaller sellers who manage inventory for other people.
  3. Learn how they work today.
  4. Find the most repetitive and costly administrative problems.
  5. Show them the existing consignment system.
  6. Test whether operators would pay for access.
  7. Run a small pilot with interested sellers.
  8. Decide which features a standalone offering needs.

What would have to change

Poda's system assumes one operator. A single admin role controls every record. A product for many operators needs each of them to have a separate workspace with its own suppliers, orders and payouts. That is a redesign of permissions, not a setting.

Orders today are created through Stripe checkout. An operator who sells through other channels needs to record sales taken elsewhere and still get the same split and statements.
